Case study · Amazon

Increasing value perception on Amazon

Customers in emerging markets shop with bank offers, coupons and no-cost EMI. The savings were real, but the product page made people work to find them. Over four years and four versions, I redesigned offers on the product page around one idea: show customers the price they will actually pay.

The problem

On the 2023 product page, savings came in many shapes: a line of text for one card, a carousel of offer types, a separate coupon row, exchange and cashback. Each offer was written as a condition, such as “Up to ₹1,000 off on ICICI debit cards” or “3 offers”, so customers had to work out which ones applied to them and whether they could be combined.

And the page never added it up. The total showed the full price. The value was there, but customers couldn’t see it, so the product felt more expensive than it was.

Customers weren’t short of offers. They were short of an answer to one question: what will I pay?

The offers landscape

Across ten markets, a single product can carry more than nine kinds of offer, from different owners: sellers, banks and Amazon itself. Simplified, they all come down to three things.

Discounts

Pay less now

  • Vendor and seller coupons
  • Gift code coupons
  • Bank card discounts

Cashback

Get money back later

  • Cashback rewards
  • Bank card cashback

Points

Earn value to spend

  • Diamonds, an Amazon currency
  • Kaiseki, another currency (not in India)
  • Shopping points on the Amazon credit card (UAE, Turkey, Saudi Arabia)

And across all three Instalment plans: interest-free or with interest, with or without a bank discount on top.

Customer insights across markets

True everywhere In emerging markets, customers are looking for ways to make products affordable. Far more than in established markets, they look for ways to bring the price down or pay in instalments. It’s why bank discounts are everywhere here, while established markets like the US have none at all.

  • India

    The most offer-dense market in the world. Customers compare apps to check prices and offers, with little loyalty to any one app.

  • UAE

    More affluent customers, who often buy several products in one go, so applying offers in the cart matters here. Instalments are paid through Tabby and Tamara.

  • Saudi Arabia

    As in the UAE, instalments are paid through Tabby and Tamara.

  • Brazil

    Instalment-heavy: even low-priced items are bought in instalments. The à vista discount for Pix, boleto and NuPay is shown as the price itself, which brings its own design constraints.

  • Mexico

    Instalment-heavy, like Brazil: even low-priced items are bought in instalments.

Principles

  1. Lead with the outcome

    Show the price after savings first. The offers that make it up come second.

  2. Do the maths for them

    Combine coupons, bank offers and EMI automatically, instead of asking customers to.

  3. Make it theirs

    Use the cards and coupons this customer already has, not generic bank offers.

Research and leadership alignment

Research

Two rounds of usability studies, in 2023–24 and 2024–26, each across five marketplaces: UAE, Saudi Arabia, India, Brazil and Mexico. Every round had 14 participants per marketplace, 7 settled and 7 unsettled customers, so we heard from both experienced and newer shoppers.

  • 2rounds
  • 5marketplaces
  • 140participants

What we found

  1. Customers understood the “Buy for” price and the offer combination straight away, and valued it.
  2. They loved the tabs, because they made instalment plans easy to discover. So the page now opens on the tab that matches each customer’s likelihood of paying in instalments.
  3. Seeing the same coupon repeated in each card caused a moment of confusion, but customers worked it out on their own within a few seconds.
  4. Customers, especially in the UAE, expect to apply coupons and offers in the cart, not on the product page. That led to a separate project, offer discovery and application in the cart.

Four versions, one idea

Each version kept what worked and fixed what the last one got wrong.

01 · 2023 · When I joined

What I inherited: offers to decode

  • Savings spread across about six different formats.
  • Offers written as conditions, not prices.
  • The total showed the full price, ₹23,999.

TakeawayThe page listed offers. It never told customers what they would pay.

02 · 2024

Show the result, not the offer

  • Offers grouped into ready-made combinations, each labelled with its result: “Buy for ₹X”.
  • Every offer type gathered behind one “See all offers & discounts” row.
  • Fewer rows competing for attention.

What we learnedLeading with the monthly EMI price pushed the real price into second place, and the total still ignored the savings.

03 · 2026 · Launched

Personal, and organised by how you pay

  • Tabs for how you pay, pay in full or No Cost EMI, each with savings already included (₹2,322 a month, down from ₹2,433).
  • Uses the customer’s own saved card (HDFC ••0424) and shows what each part saves.
  • One Apply for the whole combination.
  • Opens on the tab that suits the customer: No Cost EMI for those likely to pay in instalments.

What we learned after launchCustomers loved the tabs because they made instalments easy to discover, so we changed one thing: the tab they land on now depends on how likely they are to pay in instalments. One issue remained: the offer combination on the other tab still went unseen. The 2027 concept puts both side by side.

04 · 2027 · Concept

Apply the best bank offer in one tap

  • Bank offers are applied for you: each way to pay shows the card and coupon it uses, with its own Apply.
  • Pay in full and No Cost EMI sit side by side instead of behind tabs, so the best combination for each way to pay is visible at once.
  • Both come to ₹20,800, so No Cost EMI clearly costs nothing extra.
  • “Lowest: ₹20,300 with HSBC Credit Card” shows the best price openly, even on a card the customer doesn’t have.
  • “Buy 3, get 6% off · add 1 more” turns a quantity deal into a goal, with a progress bar.
  • No box and no nesting, so the module sits naturally in the page.

StatusA concept for 2027, building on what we learned from the 2026 launch.

Outcome

The new offers experience launched on the product page in all ten of Amazon’s emerging markets, and conversion went up. Customers now see what they’ll pay, with their own cards and coupons already counted, and can lock in that price with one tap.
